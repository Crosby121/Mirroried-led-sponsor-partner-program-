# Mirroried LED Sponsor Sales Playbook

## Opening
“Good morning, I’m looking for the person who handles marketing, partnerships, sponsorships, or local business development.”

If asked what the call is about:
“Mirroried LED builds custom illuminated infinity-mirror displays and visual products. We’re developing a limited sponsor-partner program and I’m trying to reach the person who evaluates marketing partnerships.”

## Decision-maker introduction
“Thanks for taking my call. Mirroried LED creates custom infinity-mirror and LED display products, including showroom and content-based promotional placements. I’m not calling to ask for a donation. I’m looking to see whether there’s a business partnership that makes sense for both sides.”

## Discovery questions
Ask before pitching:
- What are you trying to promote right now?
- Are you focused on awareness, local traffic, leads, product trial, content, events, or another objective?
- What types of partnerships have worked for you before?
- Do you normally work with cash sponsorships, in-kind support, or both?
- Who else needs to approve a partnership?
- What information do you need before approving one?
- Is there a target budget or contribution range?
- Are there brand, category, timing, or legal restrictions?
- How would you judge whether the partnership was worthwhile?

## Transition to offer
“Based on what you told me, I’d rather build the offer around your objective than throw a generic sponsorship package at you.”

## What the sponsor gets
Only describe inventory that can actually be delivered. Examples:
- defined showroom/display placement;
- exterior-facing campaign display during written operating windows;
- interior branded mirror/display placement;
- approved YouTube or video integration;
- campaign/event placement;
- approved social/content deliverables;
- custom branded display or demonstration;
- proof-of-performance and campaign reporting.

## Close
“Would it make sense for me to send a short proposal showing exactly what we would deliver, what we’d need from you, the campaign term, and the value on both sides?”

## Objection handling
### “Send me information.”
“Absolutely. Before I send it, what is the one thing you would need to see for it to be worth reviewing?”

### “We don’t sponsor.”
“Understood. Do you ever use local advertising, product placement, content partnerships, vendor marketing, or in-kind promotional programs? We can structure this as a marketing partnership rather than a traditional sponsorship.”

### “What’s your audience size?”
“We don’t want to overstate numbers we can’t verify. We sell the written deliverables we control and report the metrics we can document.”

### “What can you guarantee?”
“We guarantee only the placements, content, campaign dates, approved integrations, and reporting items written into the agreement.”

### “How much does it cost?”
“We have package anchors, but I first match the offer to the goal so you’re not paying for inventory you don’t need.”

### “Can you guarantee sales?”
“No. We can guarantee execution of the contracted marketing deliverables, but not customer purchasing behavior.”

## Follow-up cadence
- Day 0: proposal or requested information
- Day 2–3: concise follow-up
- Day 7: value-focused follow-up
- Day 14: final active-cycle follow-up
- Then move to nurture unless a specific next date was agreed

## CRM notes after every call
Record contact, role, objective, decision process, budget/contribution signal, objections, requested materials, next action, next date, and probability/stage.
